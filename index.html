How I work
I design systems, not just screens.

Most UX problems aren't visual problems. They're structural, organisational, or conceptual. Good design starts by understanding the system, before opening Figma.

01
Understand the system before designing screens

Before I touch a frame in Figma, I try to understand the product ecosystem. What are the core objects? How do they relate? Who are the different user roles and what do they actually need to do?

This isn't abstract. It shapes everything from navigation structure to detail page layout. A poorly understood system produces interfaces that look reasonable but don't hold up when real users and real data arrive.

02
Structure workflows around real user and business needs

A workflow is a series of decisions and actions a person needs to take to accomplish a goal. Designing it well requires knowing what that goal actually is, and what obstacles stand between the user and achieving it.

The structure follows from real needs, not from what looks good in isolation or what's easiest to build.

03
Build reusable foundations instead of isolated screens

An interface is not a collection of screens. It's a system of patterns that, when they work together, create a coherent product experience. Patterns before screens, always.

This pays off in two ways: more consistency for users, and faster future design decisions, because they become assembly, not invention.

04
Design with implementation constraints in mind

Design that can't be built isn't design. It's illustration. I work closely with engineering from early stages to make sure design decisions are grounded in what's actually possible.

This means thinking about component states, data edge cases, loading behaviours, and error states, not just the happy path.

05
Collaborate through clear decisions, documentation, and iteration

Design work that lives only in Figma doesn't reach its full potential. I document design decisions, not just the output but the rationale. Why this layout? Why this pattern? What alternatives were considered?

This creates a shared language between design, product, and engineering, and builds confidence in the decisions being made.

Partner & Admin Platform

A regulated operations platform redesigned around contract workflows, role separation, document states, and scalable product logic.

Role
UX Design Manager
Domain
Insurance · B2B Operations
Users
Partners · Admins
Focus
Workflow Systems
Overview
System design for regulated contract operations

The Partner & Admin Platform is the operational layer of the Garantiemax ecosystem. Internal teams and external partners use it to create, manage, review, and support insurance contracts. The challenge was not a visual refresh. It was giving a complex insurance workflow enough structure to be operationally reliable without removing the logic the business depends on.

System object
The contract connects customers, products, documents, statuses, and actions.
Primary users
Admins needed control and visibility. Partners needed speed, accuracy, and guardrails.
Design problem
Shared business logic had to support different role responsibilities without creating separate products.
Outcome
A clearer platform model for contract creation, contract review, and document handling.
Problem
A working platform without a working structure

Garantiemax operates in a domain where contracts, legal states, document availability, partner permissions, and internal review processes are tightly connected. A change in one area affects what users can see, edit, download, revoke, or resend across the system.

The platform had to serve both internal admin operations and external partner workflows from the same data model, but role boundaries were unclear. Admins needed deep access and control. Partners needed a narrower, guided path that reduced mistakes without exposing internal complexity. Without stronger structure, the platform could not scale to support future products, and daily operations stayed harder than they needed to be.

Key Decisions
The strongest decisions were structural
01
Admin and Partner separated by responsibility, not visual styleThe same business logic stayed connected. Permissions, actions, and depth of information changed by role, keeping one coherent system that served two different operational contexts.
02
Contract creation became a guided sequence for AdminsThe flow was structured into steps with a final overview before submission, making review and validation explicit rather than assumed.
03
Partner creation was intentionally narrowerPartners did not need the full Admin flow. A simpler path reduced room for mistakes and kept partner work focused, without requiring a separate product.
04
Contract detail became the operational hubQuick info, contract data, document controls, status logic, and role-specific actions were organised around one central object, not spread across disconnected screens.
05
Modals over side sheets for focused editsEditing contract data needed a bounded task space. A modal matched the Admin workflow better and reduced competing context, which was a deliberate tradeoff over a more spatially flexible side sheet.
System Solution
A role-aware contract platform with explicit workflow logic

The solution introduced a structured platform model across contract creation, detail views, and document handling, with distinct paths for Admin and Partner users operating from the same underlying data.

Admin flow
Multi-step creation with controlled progression and a final review before completion.
Partner flow
Simplified path for fast, accurate contract creation with fewer exposed decisions.
Contract detail
Single workspace for status, key data, documents, and available role-specific actions.
Document states
Explicit handling for current, archived, not issued, not consented, pending, and revocable documents.
Impact & Constraints
Clearer operations across roles, contracts, and documents

The redesign produced a stronger product foundation: fewer disconnected patterns, clearer role boundaries, better engineering handoff, and a platform model that supports future insurance products without rebuilding the interaction logic from scratch.

Operational clarity
Users can understand which actions are available, why some states are restricted, and where contract work continues.
Role clarity
Admin and Partner workflows are separated by responsibility and permission level while staying connected to the same contract model.
System reuse
Contract, document, table, modal, and action patterns can scale across related workflows and future product extensions.
Engineering alignment
More explicit states and flows reduced ambiguity during implementation discussions and made backend dependencies easier to surface.
Constraints that shaped the MVP
01
Backend constraints shaped the MVPThe existing backend limited how far status logic, document behaviour, and contract state handling could be changed during the redesign.
02
The platform was rebuilt progressivelyThe team duplicated the existing platform and rebuilt it with the new design system, improving hierarchy and workflow clarity while avoiding unnecessary technical debt before the planned backend migration.
03
Status redesign was intentionally deferredThe contract status system needed improvement, but changing it for the MVP would have required backend work that did not match the current roadmap.
04
Rollout was phased to reduce riskAdmins receive the MVP first so the team can validate workflows, fix issues, and improve the product before Partner access for the planned Area 30 launch.
05
Coverage was prioritised over perfectionThe MVP targets strong functional coverage across contracts, partners, guides, shop, and account settings. Detail refinements are planned for a follow up sprint after core Admin workflows are usable.

Customer App

Customer-facing insurance app simplifying complex contract logic

A guided app experience that helps customers activate, understand, and manage protection products while staying aligned with the platform’s contract and backend states.

Role
UX Lead
Type
Mobile App · B2C
Users
End customers
Status
Active / Ongoing
[ Mobile app screens — onboarding, contract overview, documents ]
Overview
Customer-facing insurance app simplifying complex contract logic

The Customer App translates Garantiemax insurance logic into a guided customer experience. Customers onboard, understand their contract, review protection details, and act on required steps, without needing to understand the operational system behind it. The contract is the central object, connecting onboarding, coverage, documents, status, and legal confirmations.

Primary user
Customers buying or activating protection products.
Core object
The contract as the customer’s source of truth.
Main challenge
Make insurance states understandable without oversimplifying legal logic.
System link
Customer states must align with platform and backend states.
Problem
Customers needed clarity at moments of uncertainty

Insurance products require trust, legal precision, and clear status communication. Customers don’t think in backend states, risk carriers, or document rules. They need to know what they bought, what is active, what is missing, and what to do next.

The app had to simplify this complexity while staying consistent with the same contract logic used by the Partner & Admin Platform. Simplification that broke that alignment would create support problems and trust issues downstream.

Key Decisions
Decisions that connected customer clarity with system reliability
01
Contract as the central objectThe app experience centres on the contract because it is the customer’s link between product, coverage, legal documents, and support, not a dashboard of unconnected features.
02
Guided onboarding over free navigationActivation steps are structured to reduce missed information and make legal confirmations explicit. Letting customers skip through freely would have broken required consent flows.
03
Backend-aligned statesCustomer UI states were designed to map back to operational states, so support teams and platform users see the same reality. This was a deliberate constraint, not just a technical detail.
04
Plain-language status communicationInsurance states were rewritten as understandable customer messages while preserving the underlying legal meaning. The translation was product work, not just copy editing.
System Solution
A clearer customer layer on top of insurance operations

The solution creates an app structure that moves customers from activation to understanding, guided where required, clear at every status point, and aligned with the same contract logic that runs the operational platform.

Onboarding
Step-based flow for activation, confirmations, and required product information.
Contract view
Central screen for coverage, status, product details, and documents.
Status logic
Customer-facing states aligned with backend and platform rules.
Legal moments
Consent and document requirements surfaced at the right point in the journey.
Impact & Constraints
A customer experience aligned with the wider ecosystem

The app creates a clearer customer layer for the same contract logic used by the operational platform, aligning product, design, and engineering around how customer states map to backend and admin workflows.

Clarity
Customers get a clear path through activation and contract review.
Consistency
App states map to platform states and support workflows.
Trust
Legal and document moments are explicit rather than hidden.
Scale
The structure supports future products and coverage types.

Constraints: legal requirements limited how much onboarding and document flows could be simplified. Customer-facing states depended on backend readiness and existing contract logic. Because the work was ongoing, measurable impact data including completion rate, support reduction, and activation time was limited at time of writing.

Raptive Design System

Reusable UI foundations for an existing product

The product was already functional. My work focused on structure, consistency, and implementation speed so the team could roll out a refreshed brand without treating every screen as a one-off design task.

Role
Product Designer
Type
Design System
Focus
Components · Specs
Context
Existing Product
Design system foundations · reusable components · rebrand rollout
Overview

A system layer for consistency and speed

Raptive needed a stronger design system foundation to support an existing product and a rebrand rollout. The goal was not to replace the product. It was to make design and development more consistent, reusable, and faster to ship. I focused on turning repeated UI decisions into shared components, documented rules, and implementation-ready specs.

Context
Existing product, active users, rebrand underway.
Risk
Solving the same interface decisions repeatedly during rollout.
Constraint
Improve consistency without forcing a product reset.
Output
Reusable components, state documentation, and dev handoff specs.
Problem

A working product with execution gaps

The product already had working flows and active users. The problem was operational: repeated interface patterns, a rebrand in progress, and handoff gaps between design and engineering were creating unnecessary rework.

Without a shared component foundation, the team would spend the rebrand resolving the same visual and interaction decisions on every screen, rather than applying them once and scaling forward.

Key Decisions

Decisions that made the system practical

01
Componentize recurring patterns firstPrioritised elements that appeared repeatedly across the product, not the most interesting ones but the ones with the most implementation value.
02
Document states, not only visualsComponents included guidance for default, hover, active, disabled, and edge cases. A component without state documentation isn't a component. It's a screenshot.
03
Design for team adoption, not Figma cleanlinessThe system had to be understandable and usable by the team in daily work. Structure and naming followed how the team worked, not idealised system architecture.
04
Use manual specs as a handoff bridgeWithout Figma Dev Mode, manual annotation sheets were the only way to make design intent legible to engineering. Tedious, but necessary and effective.
System Solution

A reusable foundation for product and brand execution

The solution combined a Figma component library with manual implementation documentation, prepared to support real development, not just design exploration.

Components
Reusable UI elements with defined structure and state logic.
Specs
Manual annotations covering spacing, states, and behaviour.
Rebrand
Brand tokens applied at component level for consistent rollout.
Governance
Shared usage rules to reduce future divergence.
Component anatomy · interaction states · implementation notes
Impact & Constraints

Faster, clearer product execution

The system gave the team a shared foundation for the rebrand and future product work, reducing repeated design decisions, improving handoff clarity, and making implementation more predictable.

Speed
Faster reuse of product patterns across screens.
Clarity
Less ambiguity during design-to-engineering handoff.
Consistency
More aligned UI decisions across the product.
Scale
Reusable foundation applicable to future product work.

Constraints: without Figma Dev Mode, all handoff relied on manual documentation, accurate but time-intensive to maintain. Long-term value depended on team adoption, which is never guaranteed by system quality alone.

Wasserfreunde Spandau 04

A digital growth project for a large sports club, connecting website UX, booking flows, social campaigns, and revenue tracking into one practical acquisition system.

Role
UX Lead · Website · Growth
Type
Club Website & Booking UX
Focus
Conversion, Content, Campaigns
Period
2025–Present
Overview

Website strategy and campaign system for a sports club

This project connected website UX, course booking flows, social campaigns, and revenue tracking into one practical growth system for Wasserfreunde Spandau 04, a large Berlin sports club with strong local relevance but fragmented digital demand.

Problem

A strong club with fragmented digital demand

Digital demand was split across website visits, social posts, phone calls, and manual booking behaviour. Parents and members moved across multiple touchpoints before committing, and the club had no clear system connecting those channels to actual bookings.

The website had to support different audiences (parents, course participants, members, prospects) while campaigns needed to move people from awareness to booking without creating operational burden for the club team.

Key Decisions

Design choices tied to measurable outcomes

01
Prioritise conversion over content volumeHigh-value actions (course booking, membership sign-up) were surfaced earlier. Users did not have to navigate through club history to reach booking intent.
02
Use social as a funnel, not decorationCampaign posts were designed around specific offers and linked to concrete actions: course pages, booking flows, membership paths.
03
Accept phone calls as part of the conversion flowObserved behaviour showed parents often discovered an offer through social, called the club for confidence, then booked. The system was designed to support this, not fight it.
04
Frame impact data carefullyRevenue figures are estimated from tracked clicks, known booking counts, and observed behaviour, not claimed as exact digital attribution. Attribution is partial and stated as such.
System Solution

A connected website and campaign engine

Website UX
Clearer structure for courses, club information, and action-oriented entry points.
Booking Flows
More direct paths from offer discovery to course and membership intent.
Social Campaigns
Posts designed around concrete outcomes, not only awareness or engagement.
Tracking Logic
Click and booking signals used to estimate impact while respecting attribution limits.
Impact & Constraints

Tracked signals showed real commercial movement

The campaigns generated tracked demand for courses and memberships. Attribution is partial. Some users moved from social discovery to a phone call before booking, so exact digital attribution cannot be claimed.

Instagram Funnel
April 2026 · 51 tracked clicks · ~38 course clicks · 20 bookings · ~5,000€ estimated revenue.
Course Campaign
~21 bookings · Anfänger 260€ · Erwachsene 260€ · Bronze 240€.
Membership Campaign
38 tracked sign-ups · ~30,000€ estimated revenue · campaign still running · final numbers pending.
Behaviour Insight
Parents often discover through social, call for confidence, then complete a booking offline or online.

Constraints: attribution is partial. Bookings were influenced by social, direct website visits, phone calls, and existing club trust. Membership campaign numbers are still running. Some conversion steps depended on staff follow-up, which cannot be tracked digitally.

Sigauri Security Service

A focused website project for a security company, built around trust, clarity, service structure, and lead generation.

Role
UX Design · Website
Type
Service Website
Focus
Trust, Clarity, Leads
Period
2025
Overview

Website strategy for a trust-driven security service

Sigauri Security Service needed a web presence that communicated services clearly and converted visitor interest into direct enquiries. The work applied the same structured product thinking used in larger platform projects, at the right scope for a client-side service business.

Problem

Security services depend on credibility before contact

Potential clients need to understand what the company offers, where it operates, and why it can be trusted, before they pick up the phone or submit an enquiry.

The challenge was structural: organise content around how buyers make decisions: services first, trust signals second, clear next step third. Avoid the generic brochure pattern where everything is vague and nothing is specific.

Key Decisions

Structure over polish

01
Service hierarchy before visual designContent was organised around what clients evaluate first: type of service, coverage area, and reliability signals, not around what looks impressive on a design presentation.
02
Direct language over marketing copyMessaging was kept specific and factual. Vague claims ("trusted professionals", "best-in-class") erode credibility for security clients who are evaluating real risk.
03
Contact path as a first-class elementThe enquiry path was made visible at multiple points rather than buried at the bottom, because the conversion goal is a conversation, not a self-serve transaction.
Impact & Constraints

A credible and more useful client touchpoint

Clarity
Services are easier to scan, understand, and evaluate.
Trust
A more professional presence that supports credibility with new clients.
Leads
Contact paths are visible and low-friction at the right moments.
Execution
Delivered a focused, client-ready website scope on time.

Constraints: no analytics baseline means impact is framed through execution quality and client readiness rather than traffic or conversion metrics. The scope was deliberately contained: the right level of structure for a service business, not a product platform.
